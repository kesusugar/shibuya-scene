# RUN 5.7 — Hybrid lower-body locomotion, proof of concept

**Verdict: the hybrid works, and I am still not integrating it — because the two gate criteria
that need the bake (RUN 4 transitions, RUN 5 foot IK in the scene) cannot be confirmed without
it, and the brief puts the bake after the gate.**

What is established: CMU 16_45's legs and Quaternius's upper body combine on one skeleton with
no visible break, and the torso lands in *exactly* the orientation the Quaternius clip puts it
— verified bone by bone, not judged by eye.

Nothing is pushed. `citizen.glb` is untouched. No BVH and no clip is committed.
241/241 tests pass (6 new); typecheck clean. RUN 6, Hit, Enter and Exit are not started.

## STEP 0 — why the CMU arms look wrong

Measured on both sides, same frames, same quantities — `scripts/cmu/arm-diagnosis.mjs`.

| | source (CMU skeleton) | retargeted (game character) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| elbow, mean | 67° | **65°** |
| elbow, minimum | 48° | **47°** |
| shoulder elevation | 1–38° | **12–49°** |
| hand above chest | −0.191 m | **−0.061 m** |

**The answer is both, and the split matters.**

- **The folded elbow is the subject's own.** 67° against 65° — the retarget reproduces it to
  within 2°. There is nothing to fix; the subject ran that way.
- **The arm *height* is the retarget.** The shoulder sits a consistent **+10°** higher and the
  hand **13 cm** higher relative to the chest, which is a rest-pose difference in the shoulder
  chain (CMU's clavicle is a zero-length helper; the character's is a real bone).

Either way the response is the same and it is what this run does: **do not use the CMU arms.**
The cause is recorded, and per the brief no time was spent fixing it.

*A correction to RUN 5.6, which called the elbow "40°":* that was the minimum across 60 samples
of `validate.mjs`. The mean is 65–67° and the range is 47–82°. A textbook running arm is nearer
90°, so the clip is still more folded than typical — but 40° overstated it.

## STEP 1–4 — the construction

`scripts/cmu/hybrid.mjs`. One skeleton, one clip, composed **offline**. There is no runtime
blending, no second mixer and no second skinned mesh — the game would load the result as an
ordinary Run.

| | bones |
| --- | --- |
| from CMU | `pelvis`, `thigh/calf/foot/ball` × 2 |
| from Quaternius | `spine_01..03`, `neck_01`, `Head`, `clavicle/upperarm/lowerarm/hand` × 2 |

**Shared phase (STEP 3).** Both sources are sampled at the same normalized gait phase with each
clip's own left-foot contact as phase zero — Quaternius's from `gaitDetail.leftContact`, CMU's
from the cycle extraction (16_45 leads with the *right* foot, so its cycle is rolled by 0.51).
Legs and arms are never on separate time axes.

**Root (STEP 4).** Vertical only, from CMU, in the pelvis's parent frame. Horizontal travel is
discarded — the controller remains the source of truth — and survives only as `stride` metadata
for RUN 4's `period = stride / speed`. A test asserts the clip cannot translate.

### STEP 5 — the hips→spine boundary, and the one quaternion that fixes it

The first hybrid **visibly hunched**. A bone's local rotation is relative to its parent, so a
Quaternius spine authored against a Quaternius pelvis, grafted onto a CMU pelvis that is
already pitched forward, compounds the two tilts.

The fix is one quaternion at the boundary bone, applied once at bake time:

```
spine_01_local  ←  (CMU pelvis)⁻¹ · (Quaternius pelvis) · spine_01_local
```

The torso then lands in the world orientation Quaternius intended while everything below stays
CMU's, and every descendant inherits the correction for free. No per-frame solver.

**It works exactly.** Mean tilt from world-up, per bone:

| variant | pelvis | spine_01 | spine_03 | neck_01 | Head |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A current Quaternius | 20° | 13° | 42° | 36° | 23° |
| B full CMU | 23° | 22° | 7° | 20° | 9° |
| C hybrid, no compensation | 23° | 16° | 46° | 40° | 26° |
| **E hybrid + boundary fix** | 23° | **13°** | **42°** | **36°** | **23°** |

E reproduces A's upper body to the degree on every bone.

## STEP 6–7 — metrics

`node scripts/cmu/compare-variants.mjs`, all at 4.2 m/s, no foot IK, one character.

| variant | step | step/ref | cadence | penetration | elbow | arm swing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A current Quaternius | 2.38 m | 1.56 | 106 | 18 mm | 92° | 722 mm |
| B full CMU 16_45 | 1.34 m | 0.88 | 188 | 0 | 66° | 251 mm |
| C hybrid (legs+pelvis) | 1.34 m | 0.88 | 188 | 0 | 92° | 708 mm |
| D hybrid (legs only) | 1.34 m | 0.88 | 188 | **158 mm** | 92° | 683 mm |
| **E hybrid + boundary fix** | **1.34 m** | **0.88** | **188** | **0** | **92°** | **684 mm** |

- **Lower body: E keeps all of CMU's gain.** step/ref 1.56 → 0.88, which is 4.6× closer to the
  1.48–1.58 m the speed and a 160–170 spm cadence imply. Penetration 18 mm → 0.
- **Upper body: E recovers all of Quaternius's.** Elbow 92° (identical to A), arm swing 684 mm
  against A's 722 and full CMU's 251.
- **D is out.** Taking the Quaternius pelvis while using CMU legs breaks the vertical
  relationship and the feet punch **158 mm** through the road.

**The legs are provably untouched**: comparing E's lower-body tracks against the full CMU clip
at 24 phases across 9 bones, the worst difference is **0.022°**. That is asserted by a test, so
it cannot drift silently.

### A metric that is not fit to separate these variants

`drift` reads 100 / 174 / 237 / 125 / 237 mm for A–E. **Ignore the differences between B, C and
E**: their lower-body tracks are identical to a hundredth of a degree, so their true drift is
identical, and the spread of ~60 mm comes from 21 keys and 25 keys landing differently on the
sampling grid at 188 spm. Three separate explanations were tried before the tracks were
compared directly — key snapping (fixed, barely moved it), loop-seam wrap (fixed, barely moved
it), threshold placement (fixed, barely moved it). The measurement is quoted here as a band,
not a figure, and the limitation is recorded in the script.

**Selected: HYBRID E, conditionally — and not integrated in this run.**

Five of the eight criteria are met on evidence and three cannot be answered without doing the
thing the gate is supposed to authorise. I am not going to call a gate passed on criteria I
have not tested, and I am not going to bake a clip into the character asset when the brief
says this run is a POC. Those three are a short, well-defined next step, not an open question.

The honest summary: **the CMU route works.** RUN 5.6 found the legs and lost the arms; RUN 5.7
gets both, and the thing that made it work is one quaternion at the hips, not a solver.

## Known limitations

- **The loop seam is real.** 16_45's cycle has 90 mm of pose discontinuity at the cut — the best
  of the CMU pool, but not zero, and it has not been hidden with a crossfade.
- **Cadence overshoots.** 188 spm against a 160–170 reference. Better than the current 106, but
  the step is 0.88 of reference rather than 1.00.
- **One subject, one trial.** 16_45 is subject 16's build and running style. Only 17 of ~2,600
  trials were examined.
- **The CMU shoulder rest offset is diagnosed, not fixed.** It does not matter while the arms
  are unused, and it would matter immediately if anyone tried to use a CMU upper body.
- **`drift` cannot separate these variants**, as recorded above.
